# Economics Contribution Audit

Use this reference to test whether a proposed contribution is real, important, and supported relative to the closest literature.

## Build the Contribution Chain

Require a complete chain:

`Prior knowledge -> unresolved uncertainty -> new evidence or model -> belief changed -> economic consequence`

If any link is missing, describe the contribution as provisional and identify what must be established.

## Classify the Contribution

Use one or more defensible categories:

- **Theory:** reveal a mechanism, proposition, equilibrium force, or boundary not captured by existing theory.
- **Measurement:** construct or validate a measure that changes an economic conclusion or decision.
- **Identification:** isolate an object that prior designs could not credibly recover.
- **Mechanism:** distinguish among competing explanations rather than add another correlation.
- **Structural or counterfactual:** identify primitives and evaluate a necessary counterfactual, equilibrium response, or welfare object.
- **Boundary or external validity:** show why a result changes under economically meaningful institutions, constraints, populations, or equilibrium conditions.
- **Distribution or welfare:** reveal incidence, heterogeneity, aggregation, or welfare consequences hidden by average effects.
- **Policy or market design:** change the interpretation, targeting, design, or expected effects of a policy or institution.
- **Descriptive fact or data:** establish a fact that constrains theory or policy; do not treat new data alone as sufficient.
- **Synthesis or reconciliation:** explain why credible findings conflict or integrate previously separated literatures.

Do not require every project to contribute in several categories. Prefer one precise contribution over several decorative claims.

## Test the Claim

Ask:

1. **Existence:** Does the proposed project actually deliver the claimed object?
2. **Relativity:** Relative to which verified closest papers is it new or different?
3. **Importance:** Why would the difference change an economic belief, decision, or interpretation?
4. **Result dependence:** Does the contribution survive a null, opposite-signed, or heterogeneous result?
5. **Design fit:** Can the proposed evidence or model support the claim?
6. **Mechanism:** Does the project distinguish a mechanism from plausible rivals?
7. **Feasibility:** Can the largest uncertainty be tested before major investment?
8. **Audience:** Which economists would update, and why?

State the strongest objection before endorsing the claim.

## Repair Weak Claims

Replace:

- "No one studies this country" with the economically meaningful institution or boundary that changes the prediction.
- "We use new data" with the previously unobservable object or test the data enables.
- "We apply a new method" with the bias, parameter, mechanism, or counterfactual the method resolves.
- "We fill a gap" with the exact unresolved uncertainty and why it matters.
- "We find a significant effect" with the belief, mechanism, distribution, welfare, or policy conclusion that changes.
- "This is the first paper" with a verified, bounded comparison or a more restrained relative claim.

## Formulate a Provisional Claim

Use this structure:

> The closest literature establishes **X** under **Y**. It does not yet establish **Z**. The proposed project uses **A** to distinguish or estimate **B**. If credible, this changes **C**.

Fill every element with source-backed content or label it as inference or unknown.

## Reach a Decision

Use one of these conditional outcomes:

- **Worth pursuing:** the question, evidence leverage, and relative contribution support a bounded pilot.
- **Promising if repaired:** a meaningful contribution may exist, but a named literature, design, measurement, or feasibility gap binds.
- **Not supported yet / park:** the current evidence does not establish importance or distinctiveness, or the required design is infeasible.

Pair the decision with the cheapest informative next step.

## Produce a Synthesis Only on Request

Do not create an RP, research plan, proposal, long contribution memo, or final positioning document unless the user explicitly requests it. Continue the dialogue otherwise.

When requested, preserve uncertainty and source hierarchy. Do not convert provisional ideas into definitive claims for the sake of a polished document.
