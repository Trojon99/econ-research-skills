# Literature Search and Evidence Audit

Use this reference to retrieve and verify literature without allowing external search to displace the user's uploaded materials.

## Enforce Source Priority

1. Read relevant current-conversation uploads completely enough to answer the live question.
2. Search the user's Zotero library for specific missing or closely related work.
3. Search online only for unresolved gaps, recent work, closest-paper checks, or verification.

Do not begin with a broad online search when relevant uploaded materials remain unread.

## Audit Uploaded Materials

For each relevant uploaded item, record internally:

- filename and bibliographic identity, if available;
- research question and economic object;
- setting, data, and sample;
- method, identification, theory, or structural model;
- principal finding or proposition;
- authors' stated contribution;
- assumptions, limitations, and unresolved margins;
- relevance to the user's current claim;
- page, section, table, or figure supporting important details when available.

Distinguish the paper's claims from the user's interpretation and from the skill's inference. If a paper cannot be fully read or text extraction is incomplete, state the limitation.

## Decide Whether Retrieval Is Necessary

Retrieve beyond the uploads only when at least one of these conditions holds:

- the user asks to find or update literature;
- a novelty, gap, or closest-paper claim depends on a broader search;
- an uploaded item cites a pivotal paper that must be checked;
- recent literature or a policy change could alter the conclusion;
- the current corpus lacks evidence for a material claim;
- conflicting claims require primary-source verification.

If none holds, continue reasoning from the uploaded corpus and mark unknowns.

## Search Zotero

Search by exact title, author, citation, keywords, mechanisms, outcomes, setting, and method. Prefer full text or detailed notes over bare metadata. Use Zotero to recover papers already curated by the user before expanding online.

Do not infer a paper's findings from its title, tags, citation context, or abstract alone when the claim requires full-text evidence.

## Search Online

Build several query families from:

- the economic question and agents;
- the proposed mechanism and rival mechanisms;
- the outcome or economic object;
- the institutional setting or policy;
- the empirical design, measurement problem, or structural counterfactual;
- titles, authors, and citations from anchor papers.

Use backward citation tracing, forward citation tracing when available, related-paper searches, recent working papers, reviews, and handbook chapters. Prefer journal pages, working-paper repositories, author or university pages, research institutions, and DOI records over unsourced summaries.

Treat search-engine snippets as discovery aids, not evidence. Open and inspect the underlying source.

## Select and Stop

Prioritize papers that are closest in question, object, mechanism, evidence, or counterfactual. Include adjacent papers only when they clarify a distinct literature strand or method.

Stop expanding when new searches mostly repeat the same anchor papers and claims, the closest-paper set is stable, and the remaining unknowns are stated. Do not call a search comprehensive unless its database coverage and procedure justify that claim.

## Maintain an Evidence Ledger

For each material claim, track:

| Field | Record |
|---|---|
| Claim | The precise proposition being used |
| Status | Source-backed / inference / unknown / disputed |
| Source tier | Upload / Zotero / online |
| Citation | Verified authors, year, title, and link or file |
| Support | Page, section, abstract, table, figure, or passage |
| Limits | Coverage, version, access, or interpretation limits |

Verify authors, year, title, version, and bibliographic details before presenting a citation. Never manufacture a DOI, URL, finding, quotation, or publication status.

When sources conflict, report the disagreement and investigate whether it reflects different settings, estimands, samples, designs, models, or versions.
